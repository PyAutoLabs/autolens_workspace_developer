ExternalShear, source = MGE bulge, 20 Gaussians per MGE.

WARNING -- THESE ARE SMOKE-TEST CONFIGURATIONS, NOT CONVERGED RUNS

Every script caps iterations / live points / eval budget so it finishes
in minutes instead of hours (see `Sampler config:` line in each summary).
The `Best fit`, `Max log L`, and `Log evidence` columns therefore reflect
where each sampler's *initial* exploration happens to land, not the true
maximum-likelihood region. Do **not** read these as scientific results.

The `Wall time`, `Sampling time`, `Likelihood evals`, and `Time per eval`

Per-call cost (the meaningful comparison)
-----------------------------------------

NumPy MGE likelihood:    ~1.4-2.4 seconds per evaluation
  - dynesty_simple:    1380 ms/eval
  - lbfgs_simple:      1416 ms/eval
  - nss_simple (cb):   1350 ms/eval
  - nautilus_simple:   2018 ms/eval
  - emcee_simple:      2394 ms/eval (also evaluates log_prior per walker)

JAX MGE likelihood (JIT-compiled):  ~0.2-0.8 seconds per evaluation
  - nautilus_jax:       188 ms/eval (Python <-> JAX boundary per call)
  - nss_jit:            207 ms/eval (no Python boundary)
  - nss_grad:           825 ms/eval (each = likelihood + jax.grad)

Headline: the JAX path is roughly **7-10x faster per likelihood call** for
the MGE imaging likelihood -- but JAX paths pay a one-shot JIT compile
cost of 54-1300 s before the first sample. That cost amortises over
thousands of evals in a real run; in these smoke tests it dominates.

JIT compile penalty
-------------------

  - nautilus_jax:    54 s compile  +  19 s sampling
  - nss_jit:        140 s compile  + 128 s sampling
  - nss_grad:      1292 s compile  +  17 s sampling   (grad path is biggest)
  - nss_simple:       <1 s compile + 1111 s sampling  (callback path; almost
                                                       no JAX work to compile)

The takeaway for a real production run: pick the JAX path, accept the
one-shot warmup, and you'll do tens-of-thousands of likelihood evaluations
~10x cheaper than NumPy. For smoke tests / quick smoke checks that only
need a handful of evals, NumPy paths win because they skip the compile.

Why the log Z values don't agree
--------------------------------

Each sampler explores a different point in the ~50-D MGE parameter space
at these tiny configurations:
  - dynesty:    starts from 30 unit-cube draws + 30 iterations
  - emcee:      starts from 30 walkers in [0.4, 0.6] of unit cube, 2 steps
  - nautilus:   starts from 50 unit-cube draws, capped at 100 evals
  - nss_simple: starts from 8 unit-cube draws, 1 NSS step
  - nss_jit:    starts from 30 unit-cube draws, 1 NSS step under JIT
  - nss_grad:   starts from 8 narrow-band draws, 1 SMC step with HMC

None of them gets past the initial exploration phase. Best-fit
parameters and log Z therefore measure "where did the random init land",
not "what is the MGE likelihood maximum". Crank `n_live`, `maxiter`,
`max_steps`, `n_like_max`, etc. for converged numbers.


===================================================================
JAX gradient-based point optimizers (MAP)  —  see gradient_optimizer_findings.md
===================================================================

Objective: -(log_likelihood + sum log_prior) minimised in the physical
parameter vector (a true MAP estimate; log_prior via autofit's jax-traceable
model.log_prior_list_from_vector). Cold-started from a perturbed prior median
(the raw median is a NaN-gradient degenerate point — ell_comps/shear at 0).
einstein_radius truth ~ 1.6.

| Optimizer              | Wall (s) | Compile (s) | Evals | ms/eval | Max log L    | Max log post | Conv |
|------------------------|---------:|------------:|------:|--------:|-------------:|-------------:|-----:|
| optax Adam      (MAP)  |    374.0 |       310.7 |   247 |   231.7 |   -158002.29 |   -158004.39 |  no  |
| optax ADABelief (MAP)  |    329.3 |       276.7 |   256 |   199.5 |   -157998.66 |   -158000.87 |  no  |
| jaxopt L-BFGS   (MAP)  |   1037.3 |       298.4 |   200 |   172.4 |   -158004.85 |   -158008.30 |  no  |

Headline
--------
Per-eval, the JAX MAP objective is ~6-8x cheaper than the NumPy MGE likelihood
(compile ~280-310 s one-shot, then ~170-230 ms/fwd+grad eval). But all three
single cold-start optimizers -- first-order AND quasi-Newton -- converge to the
SAME wrong basin: log L ~ -158000, einstein_radius driven to 4.4-5.0 against
the prior wall. L-BFGS uses an independent line search (no lr/clip), so this is
basin selection from the cold start, not a hyperparameter artifact. ADABelief
also re-enters the ell_comps/shear=0 NaN singularity after ~200 steps.

"Conv" = reached the intended optimum; all three are "no" (a converged local
optimum in the WRONG basin is not a solution). This is the failure GIGA-Lens
(multi-start) and Herculens/Enzi (warm-start chaining, SVI) are built to avoid
-- see Phase 2.

Phase 2 -- escaping the cold-start basin
----------------------------------------

| Method                     | Wall (s) | Compile (s) | Evals | ms/eval | Max log L    | r_E   | Robust?          |
|----------------------------|---------:|------------:|------:|--------:|-------------:|------:|------------------|
| multi-start Adam (12x)     |    ~1260 |       ~573* |  3600 |    ~192 |    +31787.93 | 1.600 | 2/12 -> truth    |
| numpyro SVI (ADABelief)    |    107.2 |    folded** |   800 |   134.0 |   -158022.27 | 3.54  | no (overconfident)|

*  two JIT compiles: single value_and_grad (start-filtering) + batched vmap-x12
   graph (the loop). Per single-start eval ~192 ms.
** SVI folds compile into step 1 (numpyro scan); 107 s wall includes it. SVI
   single cold-start lands in the WRONG basin too (r_E=3.54) with a tight (0.07)
   -- i.e. overconfident -- posterior: the mode-seeking VI failure Enzi et al.
   note. Every single-start method (1st-order, quasi-Newton, VI) fails here;
   only multi-start (many points) finds the truth.

WINNER: multi-start Adam. 12 broad random starts, Adam, 300 steps -> 2 land in
the CORRECT basin and recover einstein_radius = 1.600 (truth), centre (0,0),
shear ~(0.05, 0.05) at log L = +31788 -- ~190,000 nats better than every
single-start optimizer above. ~2/12 per-start success (~17%) is why one cold
start fails and twelve don't (P>=1 hit ~ 89%). This is the GIGA-Lens recipe
(Gu/Huang et al. 2022; 2.0 = arXiv:2606.30633).

See gradient_optimizer_findings.md for the full write-up.
